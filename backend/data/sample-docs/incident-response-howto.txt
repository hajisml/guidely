Incident Response How-To

Scope

This how-to covers production incidents (outages, degraded service, data
issues) and security incidents (suspected breach, leaked credentials, lost
device). When in doubt, treat it as an incident — a false alarm costs minutes,
a missed incident costs days.

Severity levels

SEV1: full outage or active security breach. Page on-call immediately, all
hands until mitigated.
SEV2: major feature broken or strong suspicion of a security issue. On-call
handles it during working hours, escalates if not mitigated within 2 hours.
SEV3: minor degradation, workaround exists. Fix within normal sprint work.

Reporting an incident

1. Post in #incidents with a one-line summary and what you observed.
2. The on-call engineer (see the rotation in PagerDuty) becomes incident
   commander, or assigns one.
3. For security incidents also email security@company.test right away. Lost
   or stolen devices must be reported within 4 hours so IT can remote-wipe.

During the incident

- The incident commander runs the response: assigns investigation, decides on
  mitigation, and posts status updates in the thread every 30 minutes for
  SEV1 and hourly for SEV2.
- Nobody communicates externally about the incident except the commander or
  whoever they delegate to. Customer-facing updates go through support.
- Do not destroy evidence: for security incidents, isolate affected machines
  instead of wiping or rebooting them.

After the incident

A blameless post-mortem is written within 5 working days for every SEV1 and
SEV2. The template lives in the wiki under Engineering > Post-mortems. Action
items get owners and due dates, and the post-mortem is shared in #incidents.

Passwords or keys leaked

Rotate the credential first, ask questions later. Then report to security as
above. API keys committed to a repo count as leaked even if the repo is
private — rotate and report within the same day.
